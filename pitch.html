Decisions, not features

FinWise's growth leaks because users never experience value tied to a real decision. Onboarding that delivers a decision-linked forecast, shared with the accountant, lifts conversion and retention and turns each accountant into a multiplier who brings in new businesses without ad spend.

The problem

FinWise pays for growth that leaks straight back out

FinWise has product-market fit and $10M ARR, and is betting the next year on a product-led reverse trial. The numbers show an engine that needs more money every quarter just to stand still.

2%

of trials convert to paid

Between 1.87% and 2.08% every month for 13 months, even with full premium access.

40%

of paying customers stay a year

Three in five churn within twelve months, so revenue has to be refilled constantly.

Ads

carry most of the acquisition budget

Paid sign-ups flow into a trial that doesn't activate them, so they leak out too.

The question for the next 12 months: where does the experience fail to create value, and how do we turn a leaky engine into one that compounds without leaning on paid acquisition?

What I read

A Ceiling is a product limit, not a funnel problem. Pushing users into existing features won't break it; a new value moment will. Sessions, import and modeling are vanity metrics for FinWise.

What the data can't tell me

Whether price contributes at the downgrade, and whether a decision-linked forecast predicts conversion. Neither is in the dataset, so both are tested, not assumed.

The bet · Module 1

Value is a decision answered, not a feature used

Trial users have everything but never reach an outcome that matters to their business. The value moment is a forecast tied to a real decision: a new contract, a hire, a tax payment. Those decisions are usually reviewed with an accountant, so the value moment is naturally collaborative.

Qualitative signal: an interview with a senior accountant serving SMBs pointed to forward-looking forecasting, not bookkeeping, as the most valuable capability. Treated as a hypothesis (n=1) and backed by a four-tool review benchmark later in this deck.

What I'm deliberately not doing

  • No more acquisition spend. The problem is value, not traffic; I accept flat trial volume short term.
  • No price or discount changes first. They would bury the activation effect and attract low-fit users.
  • No referral rewards or content loops yet. Referral needs satisfied customers FinWise doesn't have yet.
  • No experiment on conversion directly. At ~500 trials a month, a 2% → 2.5% lift would take years to detect.
Growth hypothesis Activation → Revenue drop-off, same root cause in Retention4
How we measure · Aim, move, prove

One North Star, two levers to move, revenue to prove it

Aim
Monthly decision-active accounts

Accounts where, this month, the owner saved or updated a decision with their own numbers, or an accountant reviewed one. Can't be inflated by clicks.

Move: what experiments target
  • Decision-forecast rate within 7 days. Share of new trial users who save a forecast for a real decision with their own numbers.
  • Time to first value. Sign-up to that first saved decision; non-completers reported separately.
  • Collaborative-account rate. Owner plus at least one active accountant or partner.
Prove: earned, not moved
  • Trial-to-paid conversion
  • 90-day paid retention
  • Retention by plan at 6 and 12 months; annual renewal rate, never blended
  • MRR

Acquisition is judged by cost per activated account by channel, not cost per sign-up. Import is kept as a diagnostic only: it is setup, not value.

Growth loop · Collaboration

The accountant is the multiplier that replaces ad spend

One accountant bringing ten clients is one round of expansion. It becomes a loop only when those businesses invite collaborators of their own, including other accountants.

The insight to test

Accountants spread FinWise because it improves their own work and client relationships. No referral rewards, so the test shows real motivation.

How we'll know it works

  • Reach: % of invited accountants who bring in another business.
  • Value: % of those businesses that save their first decision. Invitations alone don't count.
Hard side accountants: supply the review owners value, each serves many clients7
Retention · Module 3

The fiscal calendar gives FinWise a reason to bring users back

A retained customer pays and makes a decision with FinWise, or shares one with their accountant. FinWise's natural rhythm is monthly, not daily, so DAU/MAU is the wrong metric.

RhythmDecisionHow FinWise brings users back
MonthlyCash planning, payrollProgress bar: the next decision to make "Ready"
QuarterlyVAT and tax paymentsForecast triggered 2–3 weeks before each deadline
OccasionalNew contract, a hireSaved forecasts flagged when something real changes

Spain, 2026: quarterly VAT (modelo 303) and prepayments are due 20 April, 20 July, 20 October and 30 January. Every SMB faces them at the same time.

"Plan before the rush"

"Review your estimated VAT payment and cash position, then share it with your accountant." Sent early, because accountants are at their busiest in the final days.

Switching cost that builds over time

Each saved decision stores the business's costs, payment patterns and the accountant's review. Leaving means losing a shared history, an organisational cost, not a personal one.

Floor before ceiling fix customer retention first; net dollar retention later8
Market evidence · qualitative triangulation

Competitor reviews tell the same story: ease attracts, value keeps

I reviewed customer reviews of four SMB finance tools on G2 and Capterra: Wave, Quipu and Holded (Spain), and Xero (G2 theme summary across 1,845 reviews, plus its 1–3 star reviews).

  • Ease and price attract, but don't retain. Even unhappy Xero reviewers praise ease of use; they leave over limits, support and reliability.
  • Forward-looking decisions are underserved. Wave has no forecasting, Holded's cash-flow analysis is criticised, Quipu's projections are praised.
  • In Spain, the gestor is part of the product. Quipu users value advisor access; Holded users describe costly owner–gestor errors.
  • Trust failures cause the angriest churn. Frozen payouts, blocked access, features taken back from free plans.

What this does and doesn't show

Directional evidence from other products' customers: small, self-selected samples, partly incentivised reviews, mostly paying users rather than trial non-converters.

It supports the diagnosis and sets guardrails. It doesn't prove the Aha or the accountant loop. Only FinWise's own experiments can.

The first experiment

Ship only if activation rises 10 points without people dropping off

Hypothesis

If we replace generic onboarding with a decision-led flow for new trial users, the decision-forecast rate within 7 days rises by at least 10 points, because value comes from a decision answered, not feature activity.

Detectable (MDE)~8 points at a 25% baseline · ~500 users per group · 80% power, p < 0.05
Worth shipping+10 points, significant, reported with a 95% confidence interval
GuardrailDay-1 drop-off no more than +5 points vs control; checked weekly
Read date8 weeks of enrolment + 7 days; primary metric read once
Proves valueTrial-to-paid and 90-day retention, read on their own dates

Decision rules, set before launch

  • Clear win ≥ +10, significant, guardrails fine: staged rollout
  • Modest win significant but below +10: iterate on the inputs and retest
  • Inconclusive interval crosses zero: not proof it fails; gather more evidence
  • Mixed signal activation up, drop-off up > 5: investigate screen by screen
  • False peak strong early, fading by weeks 7–8: don't ship on novelty

Known limitation: the new flow makes the shared outcome easier to reach than importing data, so part of a lift could be a simpler task. That's why conversion and retention, not activation alone, confirm value.

Conversion directly? 2% → 2.5% would need ~13,800 users per group: over four years of traffic11
Monetization · Module 6

Don't fix a value problem with price. Move the paywall to the decision.

Stage 1, Value Creation, moving into Stage 2. Subscription with the reverse trial kept; monthly or annual (annual discounted). One structural bet: what the free tier keeps when the trial ends.

Free, after the trial

Keep what you've seen

Saved decision forecasts stay visible, read-only. The quarterly VAT estimate stays free and brings users back every deadline.

Paid

Act on the decision

Create, update and compare forecasts; share and review with the accountant. Users hit the paywall when they need to act.

Later

Accountant plan

Multiple clients in one plan: the route to net dollar retention above 100%, once the floor holds.

Model signal

Upgrades cluster when users try to update or share a forecast, often near a tax deadline; cohorts keep paying through billing cycles 2–3; paid conversion doesn't fall.

Not tested yet, on purpose

Price points, discounts, a collaboration bundle and a VAT-only plan are Stage 3. The VAT-only plan becomes a test only if data shows a stable VAT-only segment.

Key trade-off a more generous free tier builds trust and returns, at the risk of some users never paying12

Prove value first, then let the loop replace ad spend

Months 1–3
Measure and activate
  • Track decisions at user level; set the baseline
  • Check that saving a decision predicts conversion
  • Run the onboarding A/B test
Months 4–6
Roll out and retain
  • Roll out the flow if it's a clear win
  • Start the accountant-loop holdout
  • Start fiscal triggers ahead of the next quarterly tax deadline
Months 7–9
Monetize what works
  • Launch the paywall change for new cohorts
  • Read retention and loop results at 90 days
  • Test "example first" vs "form first"
Months 10–12
Shift the engine
  • Read paywall cohorts over 2–3 billing cycles
  • Move budget from ads to the accountant channel and deadline content
  • Decide on the accountant plan

Scale when

A test hits its threshold with guardrails intact, and the lagging read confirms it. Ad budget moves channel by channel, judged by cost per activated account.

Stop or rethink when

Activation rises but conversion doesn't follow, accountants invite but their clients never decide, or free users don't return after the trial.

The story

What building this taught me

The friction

The metric I needed didn't exist in the data. FinWise only tracked feature activity, which had doubled without moving conversion. I defined a new value metric, a decision answered, built it into the prototype's tracking, and stated openly that it can only be validated once FinWise tracks it at user level.

The Aha

The product doesn't have to wait for the user. Spanish tax deadlines hit every SMB at the same time, so FinWise can trigger value itself: a reason to return, a moment to involve the accountant, and an upgrade moment in one.

What stuck

  • Experimentation gave structure to how I've worked for years.
  • It must start with customer value: a better metric isn't a better product.
  • A disproved hypothesis is still progress.
  • Discipline (method, thresholds, read date up front) makes results trustworthy.
  • Each experiment informs the next.

Decisions, not features: the same value moment drives activation, retention, monetization and, through the accountant, acquisition.
